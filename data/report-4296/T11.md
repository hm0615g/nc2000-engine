# T11: search diagnostics and a withdrawn allocation candidate

The subsequent [selection and backpropagation trace](t11-cause/README.md) identifies a concrete contributing mechanism: Hidden Power's more fragmented continuation faces more opponent exploration, and the resulting opponent mistakes are credited to Hidden Power at the root. It includes an exact-state one-step intervention and verifies that observation preserves the original search. The extent of that mechanism's contribution to the whole ranking remains to be isolated; no corrective policy is introduced.

Splitting the same 30,000 simulations into 30 independent 1,000-simulation searches, then selecting the action with the largest **sum of root visits**, chooses Earthquake on all 32 held-out T11 seeds. The current single-tree policy chose Hidden Power on all 64 seeds in the original audit and all eight additional diagnostic seeds. This is a reproducible action change toward the reference-preferred move, but it does not identify or repair the cause of the original decision. The candidate has been withdrawn; its research harness and measurements remain for reproduction.

The original terminal-continuation comparison supports targeting Earthquake: 332/1,024 reference wins versus 253/1,024 for Hidden Power. The smaller OpenAgent continuation sample was 81/256 versus 73/256 and did not independently establish that difference. These existing conditional comparisons must not be relabeled as an arena win-rate improvement for the ensemble. The subsequent [regression experiment](../ensemble-regression-v1/README.md) was stopped at the user's request before its fixed sample completed, and its agent integration was reverted. No completed full-game strength gate supports this candidate.

## What the diagnostics establish

Simply spending more on one tree did not fix the decision: at 120,000 simulations, both tested seeds chose the Ampharos switch. Removing unobservable damage history from the key also left Hidden Power at 8/8. Zero rollout exploration, fully random rollout exploration, zero extra rollout turns and 100 extra rollout turns each left Hidden Power at 2/2. None of these is a successful T11 candidate.

The leaf trace uses the production rollout function and preserves the ordinary action statistics exactly on the checked seed 62001. Across seeds 62001–62008, in the final quarter of the 30,000-simulation search, Earthquake's traced leaves begin on average 3.82 turns beyond the root and Hidden Power's 4.03 turns beyond it. Their subsequent rollouts reach terminal outcomes 98.66% and 89.92% of the time respectively. The separate 100-turn rollout trace reaches terminal outcomes on every traced sample in the final quarter and still chooses Hidden Power. Thus static cutoff evaluation alone cannot explain this failure. The short tree plus its simple continuation policy can rank actions incorrectly even when the continuation reaches the end of the battle.

Terminal branches reached inside the tree bypass the leaf callback and are excluded from these leaf percentages. Trace entries are keyed by `action|quarter|terminal|own survivors|opponent survivors`; each value is `[count, own reward sum, tree depth in turns sum]`. Quarters are zero-indexed. These diagnostic rewards are intentionally kept separate from the independent terminal-continuation audit.

Reducing the ordinary search to 1,000 simulations selected Earthquake 6/8, Hidden Power 1/8 and Rock Slide 1/8. In this position, allocating more simulations to one growing tree worsens the root decision. Pooling independent shorter trees reduces the seed variation without following the same deepening trajectory. That observed behavior motivates the ensemble; it does not prove that shorter trees are better generally or identify a single faulty rollout move.

An alternative replaced each rollout decision with a small full-information search while keeping the outer tree at 1,000 simulations. The 16-simulation version initially chose Earthquake 2/2 but only 10/32 on held-out seeds, with Hidden Power 17, Rock Slide 4 and Swords Dance 1. The 64-simulation version initially chose Earthquake 2/2 and then chose Earthquake 9/16, Rock Slide 5 and Hidden Power 2. The ordinary 1,000-simulation control already frequently chooses Earthquake, so the first two successes do not isolate a benefit from nested search. Neither nested candidate is promoted.

## Artifacts and reproduction

`t11-followup.jsonl` contains 247 compacted decision records, including unsuccessful screens, leaf diagnostics and every ensemble member. `t11-followup.summary.json` gives per-cohort counts and SHA-256 hashes of the original scratch JSONL files. Hidden Power and Hidden Power Bug input spellings are normalized only in the summary counts. Screen, diagnostic and held-out cohorts remain separate.

```sh
cargo build --release -p nc2000-bot --example reported_decisions
cargo test --release -p nc2000-bot --test report_4296

target/release/examples/reported_decisions \
  --log data/report-4296/battle.raw.log \
  --own-team data/report-4296/own-team.json \
  --opponent-team data/report-4296/opponent-team.json \
  --profile open --turns 11 --seed 65001 --seeds 32 \
  --trees 30 --out tmp/report-4296-ensemble-rerun
```

Omit `--trees 30` for the ordinary policy. Use `--trace-leaves` to observe the unchanged rollout, `--eps 0` or `--eps 1` for rollout exploration controls, and `--iters 1000 --leaf-search-iters 16` for the nested-search alternative. Nested decisions use the default full-information SkuctSearch, with both sides selected from the same simultaneous search; its rollout horizon is still controlled by `--rollout-turns`. These switches are confined to the research example. The production rollout function is exposed for instrumentation; its implementation and all production defaults remain unchanged.
