# Screenshot party versus the shipped Nash mixture

The submitted party uses level-55 Snorlax and Raikou with level-50 Cloyster,
Skarmory, Misdreavus, and Gengar. The owner changed Misdreavus's Miracle Berry
in the screenshot to Mint Berry to satisfy Item Clause. All other visible sets
are transcribed verbatim in `party.json`.

Raikou's supplied BDFF DVs mean Attack 11, Defense 13, Speed 15, Special 15,
and derived HP 15: Hidden Power Ice, base power 70. Other DVs, stat experience,
and happiness use maximum values. There are 16 legal three-Pokemon subsets
under the level-155 cap. Both the bundled wasm validator and the native
validator accept this party. Native serialization drops the cosmetic nature
field, so its validation output contains nature-normalization findings only.

## Conditions

- Both players use `BlindAgent` with equal per-decision iteration budgets,
  the shipped 87-team `belief-pool-v1`, no baked preview tables, and no pondering.
- The opponent is sampled from the shipped `meta-nash-v1/pool-artifact.json`:
  sample-07/08/10 with normalized weights 0.575/0.998, 0.222/0.998, 0.201/0.998.
- Each independent block draws one opponent and battle seed, then plays two
  games with P1/P2 reversed. Agent seeds follow the existing `prior_exploit`
  schedule. Both agents choose their own preview selection and all battle moves.
- Main measurement: 1,024 games at 3,000 iterations, seed 2026092701.
- Budget sensitivity: 256 games at 10,000 iterations, fresh seed 2026092702.
- Games reaching the harness's 500-turn limit receive 0.5, as do engine ties.
  Raw logs distinguish these two outcomes.
- The submitted party is not added to either agent's belief pool.

## Reproduce

From the repository root, run:

```sh
bash data/party-vs-nash-20260927/run.sh
```

The script builds the native harness, validates the party, records source/input/
binary SHA-256 fingerprints in `manifest.json`, and resumes missing games.
It refuses to mix changed inputs or a changed binary into an existing run.
Reproduction after changing the toolchain or code requires a fresh output directory.
The `smoke/` files record four identical games at one and four worker threads.
A separate invalid duplicate-item input was rejected with exit status 1.

## Interpretation

`results/*.jsonl` and the harness summaries score the Nash side (`X`).
`summary.json`, generated by `summarize.py`, reverses this to the submitted
party's perspective: win 1, draw or turn cap 0.5, loss 0. The overall score
estimates performance against the randomly sampled mixture; it is not an
unweighted average of the three opponent-specific rates.

Reported 95% intervals use the independent two-game block means and the
normal approximation, mean +/- 1.96 * sample standard deviation / sqrt(blocks).
The predeclared primary result is the 3,000-iteration mixture score: lower
bound above 0.5 means a demonstrated winning matchup; upper bound below 0.5
means a demonstrated losing matchup; otherwise the result is unresolved.
The 10,000-iteration run tests sensitivity to search budget, not an alternative
primary endpoint. These results concern this bot, prior, and opponent mixture;
they do not certify performance against every possible metagame opponent.

## Results

| Iterations | Games | Party wins | Party losses | Win rate | Paired 95% CI |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 3,000 | 1,024 | 322 | 702 | 31.4% | 28.5–34.3% |
| 10,000 | 256 | 67 | 189 | 26.2% | 20.5–31.8% |

No draws or turn caps occurred. The predeclared primary endpoint establishes
a losing matchup against the mixture; increasing the search budget does not
reverse that conclusion. The difference between budgets itself is not claimed
as statistically established. All three opponent-specific point estimates
are below 50% at both budgets. See `summary.json` for paired intervals and
per-opponent counts, and `REPORT.txt` for the Japanese copy-ready report.
